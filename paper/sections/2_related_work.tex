\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reliability of LLM judges.}
The LLM-as-a-judge paradigm was popularized by MT-Bench, which reported
human-comparable agreement alongside systematic biases---position,
verbosity, and self-enhancement~\cite{zheng2023judging}. The critique
literature that followed is large; we organize it by what it perturbs
and what consequence it measures. \emph{Instance-level critiques}
perturb the judgment call itself: position and verbosity
biases~\cite{zheng2023judging}, self-preference~\cite{selfpref2026},
non-determinism at temperature zero and order flips~\cite{allverdicts2026},
sensitivity to prompts and rubrics~\cite{hassell2026mawile}, and the
elicitation format itself---forced single-label ratings on indeterminate
tasks bias judge validation by as much as 30\%~\cite{guerdan2025validating}.
\emph{Ranking-level critiques with a human ground truth} then ask what
these instabilities do to system rankings: JuStRank~\cite{gera-etal-2025-justrank}
benchmarks 48 judges as system rankers against a Chatbot Arena ground
truth, finding that instance-level judge quality does not guarantee
correct system ranking and characterizing per-judge system bias and
``decisiveness''; GAUGE~\cite{bodhwani2026gauge} shows that an
LLM-simulator plus LLM-judge gate for task agents picks the lower-reward
agent on 31\% of near-equal pairs (under 1\% for far-apart pairs) and
that satisfaction does not track task success; and in information
retrieval, LLM-generated relevance labels move 78--83\% of system
ranking positions~\cite{otero2024limitations}. Bencher-design work
varies the evaluation model as one component of an automatic ranking
pipeline and finds rankings move with it~\cite{gao-etal-2025-evaluating}.
The largest audits generalize across benchmarks and find reliability is
item-specific rather than model-level~\cite{reliability2026,
allverdicts2026}. Our paper occupies the one cell this literature leaves
open: the quantity of interest is a \emph{component-importance
conclusion} drawn from an ablation, there is \emph{no human ground
truth}, and the comparison holds transcripts \emph{identical} so that
judge identity is the only changed factor. Three distinctions from the
closest neighbors: the ranked objects in JuStRank and
\cite{gao-etal-2025-evaluating} are independent systems with different
outputs, whereas ours are component variants of one system; their
reference is a human ground truth, whereas we ask whether the conclusion
is stable across judges at all; and GAUGE's gate can appeal to a
verifiable task reward, which open-ended discussion quality does not
have. (GAUGE also reports family favoritism in its setting while we do
not detect self-preference in ours; the discrepancy is consistent with
the item-specific nature of judge behavior~\cite{allverdicts2026} and
with our study's low power on that contrast.)

\paragraph{Mitigations: panels and escalation.}
Two mitigation families are established. Panels of diverse models
(``juries'') aggregate judgments so that individual biases
cancel~\cite{verga2024juries}, and escalation schemes escalate
low-agreement cases to humans with statistical
guarantees~\cite{jung2024trust}. Both are recommendations for
evaluation practice; neither has been validated for the
multi-agent-discussion artifact, and neither measures what happens to
published component rankings when panels replace single judges. Our
audit provides exactly that measurement, and our proposed reporting norm
operationalizes the escalation idea at the level of conclusions rather
than items.

\paragraph{Evaluation of multi-agent systems.}
Multi-agent debate improves reasoning and factuality in task-oriented
settings~\cite{du2023debate}, and frameworks such as MALLM make debate
components configurable with integrated evaluation
pipelines~\cite{becker-etal-2025-mallm}. Surveys of agent-as-a-judge
catalog single-model, multi-agent, and agentic
evaluators~\cite{agentasjudge2025}. Closest to our object of study,
\emph{Who Speaks Next?} applies conversation-analytic norms
(adjacency pairs, self-selection from internal states) to speaker
control in multi-party LLM dialogue and evaluates the result with LLM
judges~\cite{whospeaks2024}; voice-agent research learns turn-taking
decisions with chunk-level ground truth and validates them with
deterministic metrics plus a single auxiliary judge
model~\cite{moderatorlm2026}; and asynchronous group communication
studies when an agent should speak, using human-anchored
metrics~\cite{eckhaus-etal-2025-time}. None of these works varies the
\emph{judge} as an experimental factor, and none reports structural
discussion metrics (speaking inequality, responsiveness) or
task-outcome anchoring. Our study supplies the missing factor and the
judge-free metrics.

\paragraph{Reproducibility audits as a genre.}
Audits of published results are an established publication form:
reproducibility studies of evaluation
pipelines~\cite{wang2024canyoutrust}, reporting standards for
reproducible LLM evaluation, and venue-level replication programs all
treat the verification of a field's methods as a first-class
contribution. This paper follows that genre: the audited object is not a
model's capability but an evaluation \emph{protocol}, and the artifact
under audit is the audited framework's own published-style evaluation.
