\section{Auxiliary model review record}
\label{app:jury}

Because the authors audited their own system's evaluation, an
independent model (different family from the authors' toolchain)
reviewed the evidence-to-claim mapping before this manuscript was
finalized; this is an auxiliary review by a model outside the authors'
toolchain, not a blind review and not an independent replication. The jury received the full results, the candidate claims,
the known caveats, and instructions to avoid inflating claims beyond
the data. Its verdict was \emph{partial} support with \emph{medium}
confidence. Points the jury raised and how this manuscript responds:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{``Non-significance under small $n$ is a design property,
not a finding.''} Accepted: the manuscript's primary evidence for RQ3
is the direction reversal with maximum effect size and intervals for
the differences; exact $p$-values are reported as auxiliary and no
significance claim is made for any single contrast.
\item \emph{``Self-preference non-detection is a power statement, not
proof of absence.''} Accepted: claims are stated as ``not detected''
with intervals and an exact permutation $p$-value, never as absence.
\item \emph{``Driver capability is partly confounded with judge identity
in the factorial.''} Accepted: the same-machine replication
(\S\ref{sec:results}) holds transcripts fixed and varies only the
judge, the confounding is stated in \S\ref{sec:discussion}, and no
driver-independent causal claim is made beyond the tested transcripts.
\item \emph{``The setting is narrow.''} Accepted: the posture is a
protocol audit case study; generality is claimed for the inferential
move (single-judge component ranking), not for the framework.
\end{itemize}

The full jury transcript, the deterministic evidence pre-check, and
the claim-by-claim responses are released with the repository.

\paragraph{Parser-robustness sensitivity.}
Because the 5.0 default enters the reported overall scores, we re-ran
the headline analyses on a parser-clean subset: parse-fallback
dimensions treated as missing, a discussion--judge cell kept when at
least three of five dimensions were directly parsed (30, 29, and 30 of
32 cells retained per judge; script and output released). The clean
overall is a complete-case estimand (the mean of the directly parsed
dimensions, at least three of five), not a corrected estimate of the
five-dimension score; the comparison quantifies sensitivity, not
validation. The leniency ladder is preserved, with intervals still
excluding zero for the two
extremes ($+0.63$ $[+0.46,+0.79]$, $+0.03$ $[-0.27,+0.34]$, $-0.66$
$[-0.86,-0.47]$). The same-model judge's direction reversal
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) is preserved on both contrasts. One of the
six p2 direction labels moves---the cross-vendor judge's no-jitter cell
flips to full-below with only three of five discussions retained---so
direction claims for that judge are treated as suggestive. Rank
agreement remains low for two pairs ($\rho=+0.03$, $-0.09$) but one
cross-vendor pair rises from $+0.28$ to $+0.61$ (moderate) on the clean
subset, while mean absolute differences stay large (0.73--0.84). We
therefore state the agreement finding as heterogeneous and modest
rather than uniformly near-zero, and the sensitivity numbers above
show how much the parser fallback moves each claim.

\section{Robustness re-judgment of the turn-count defect}
\label{app:robust}

The audited judge prompt's pre-computed speaking-count statistic omits
agents that never speak (\S\ref{sec:method}), affecting 7 of the 32
discussions: \texttt{p1-step-5-preview-t2-r1},
\texttt{p2-full-r1/r2/r3}, \texttt{p2-no\_jitter-r3},
\texttt{p3-no\_jitter-r1/r3}. We re-judged exactly these discussions
with corrected statistics (all three agents counted, true maximum gap),
under the same three judges, prompts, and temperature.
Table~\ref{tab:robust} reports the effect. The corrected re-judgment
did not overturn any qualitative direction: under corrected statistics
the same-model judge still ranks the full system below both ablations
(cell means $6.96$ vs.\ $7.04$ and $7.40$), the cross-vendor judge
still ranks it above both ($6.68$ vs.\ $6.16$ and $6.04$), and the
same-machine orderings are unchanged. Because the re-judgment is a
fresh sample at temperature 0.1, the observed score shifts---including
the $+1.00$ average rise on the flagship judge's previously capped
dimensions---cannot be attributed solely to the prompt correction;
they bound the defect's plausible influence rather than identifying its
causal effect. The main analysis therefore keeps the audited prompts,
and this appendix establishes that the reported directions survive the
correction. (A separate sensitivity analysis on the parser-clean
subset, \S\ref{app:quality}, moves one cross-vendor direction label;
both checks are reported so neither is read as stronger than it is.)
